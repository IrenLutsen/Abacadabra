What is mental maths, and what does it give your child?

Mental maths (also called mental arithmetic or abacus maths) is a way of calculating with a Japanese abacus, the soroban. First children move the beads with their hands; over time they picture the abacus and calculate in their head. Children can start from age 5–6.

  • Mental calculationworking sums out in their head using a picture of the abacus
  • Number confidenceones, tens and hundreds become visible on the beads
  • A practice habit10 minutes a day – a small routine children can do on their own
  • Focus on the taskin lessons children practise staying with one problem until it's done

What the research says. In a three-year randomised study of 204 children aged 5–7, mental abacus training improved arithmetic skills compared with a control group. Results depended on regular practice over a long period (Barner et al., 2016).

We only talk about what the method actually trains: calculation and confidence with numbers. No “whole-brain” claims, no IQ promises, no guaranteed grades.

Parents' questions about mental maths

What is mental maths?

It's a method of mental calculation using a soroban (Japanese abacus), also called mental arithmetic or abacus maths. Children first calculate on the abacus with their hands, then picture it and calculate in their head.

Does my child need mental maths?

It helps if you'd like your child to calculate confidently and worry less about sums. It doesn't replace school maths – it complements it. The free trial lesson will show whether the method suits your child.

How can I teach my child to calculate quickly in their head?

Regularity works best: a little practice every day and a gradual move from objects to imagination. In mental maths that object is the abacus: children see the number on the beads, then “move” them in their mind. With us that's 1–2 lessons a week and 10 minutes on the trainer a day.

What age can children start mental maths?

From age 5. For five-year-olds we start with a trial lesson: your child should count to 10 and be able to work with an adult online for 20–30 minutes. Most children start between 6 and 10.

Does my child need any preparation?

No. Knowing numbers up to 10 is enough – we explain everything else from scratch.

How long until we see results?

Most children understand how numbers “look” on the abacus within the first few lessons. Fluent mental calculation builds with regular practice over a year or more.

What is a soroban, and where do I buy one?

A soroban is a Japanese abacus: the top bead on each rod is worth 5 and each of the four lower beads is worth 1. You'll need one with at least 13 rods. After the trial lesson we'll send you a list of where to buy one in your country.

Can we join from anywhere in the world?

Yes. Lessons are live on Zoom, so you can join from the UK, the US, Germany, Poland, Spain or anywhere else. We schedule lessons around your time zone.

Which language are lessons taught in?

English or Ukrainian – just choose in the booking form. We have English-speaking teachers.

How is this different from school maths?

Mental maths doesn't replace school maths; it trains one specific skill – calculation, first on the abacus and then in your head. It works well alongside school.
